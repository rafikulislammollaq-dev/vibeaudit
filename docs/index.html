Ten checks. Every one of them has broken a real app.

Run automatically, judged by a human, explained without jargon.

01

Committed secrets

Is your .env in git? Has the database admin key ever been shipped to a browser?

02

Public database access

Your database URL and "public" key are meant to be public. I test whether strangers can actually read your tables with them.

03

Row-level security

Does every table actually restrict each customer to their own rows — or did the rule never get written?

04

Elevated database functions

Functions that run with database-admin rights are the classic way one customer reads another customer's data.

05

Who is actually signed in

Are admin pages and edge functions checking the person making the request, or trusting what the browser tells them?

06

Debug leftovers

Test routes, verbose errors and source maps that should never have shipped.

07

Token handling

Tokens that are decoded instead of verified, or accepted with a "none" algorithm.

08

Cross-origin access

Can any website in the world call your API and read the response?

09

Missing browser protections

The headers that stop the simplest attacks, and whether your site can be framed by another site.

10

Known-risky dependencies

Anything in your package list with a published advisory — and whether it is actually used.

How it works

No sales calls. No 30-minute discovery. Four steps, 48 hours.

  1. You send access. A repo link, a zip, or your live URL. No credentials needed for most checks — and I never keep a copy of your code.

  3. I read the results. A scanner finds things. A person decides which ones matter and whether the fix will break your app.
  4. You get the report. Plain-English findings, ranked by what would hurt you most, each with a fix you can paste in. Plus a short walkthrough video.

Questions

Will you tell me to rewrite everything?

No. Most findings are one or two lines. The report tells you what to change and what to leave alone, ranked by what actually matters for your app.

What do you need from me?

A repo link with read access, a zip, or the live URL plus your explicit permission to test it. For database checks I may ask you to run one read-only query yourself — I never ask for production credentials.

Is this legal to do to my own app?

Yes — it is your app and you authorise every check. I never test a system without written authorisation, and the live checks send a single harmless read-only request.

Will you find something embarrassing?

Probably. That is the point, and nobody else sees it. The report is written to be handed straight to whoever needs to act on it.

What if there is a real, exploitable vulnerability?

I will tell you immediately and separately, ahead of the report, with the fix first. If it is being exploited right now, drop everything and rotate your keys first.

Do I need to fix everything before launch?

No. You get a ranked list so you can decide. Most teams fix the critical and high items and launch.

Can you help after the review?

Yes — that is the Rescue tier. I implement the fixes and re-scan to confirm, and you can go from Sweep to Rescue at any point.
